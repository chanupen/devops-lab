# Lab 3: SLO and burn-rate alerts for the sample app

An SLO says how reliable a service must be. An alert on it should page you when users are hurt fast enough to matter, and stay quiet otherwise. In this lab you pick an SLI for the sample app, set a 99.5% SLO over 30 days, write the multiwindow, multi-burn-rate alerts from the Google SRE workbook, make them fire on purpose, and compare your rules with the ones Sloth generates.

The lab runs on your main lab's cluster, in the namespaces `lab03-slo` (rules) and `lab03-load` (traffic). It needs the `obs` and `sample-app` stacks (`lab up`). Prometheus loads PrometheusRules from every namespace, so rules in `lab03-slo` are picked up within about a minute.

You need `kubectl`, `jq`, `curl` and `podman`. Sloth runs as a pinned image through rootless Podman. To use the Prometheus and Alertmanager UIs, port-forward them in a second terminal:

```bash
kubectl -n monitoring port-forward svc/kps-prometheus 19090:9090 &
kubectl -n monitoring port-forward svc/kps-alertmanager 19093:9093 &
```

Then open `http://127.0.0.1:19090` and `http://127.0.0.1:19093` (from the Mac, forward those ports with `ssh -L` too).

Check each step with `lab labs verify`. `lab labs hint` gives one hint at a time. `lab labs solution N` shows a reference answer.

## Lab windows and production windows

The Google SRE workbook alerts on how fast the error budget burns, each over a long window (is enough budget gone to matter?) and a short one (is it still burning?):

| Alert | Burn rate | Budget spent | Production windows | Severity | Lab windows |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| fast burn | 14.4x | 2% | 1h and 5m | page | 10m and 2m |
| slow burn | 6x | 5% | 6h and 30m | page | 1h and 10m |
| budget burn | 1x | 10% | 3d and 6h | ticket | 12h and 1h |

With production windows the fast burn needs up to an hour of errors, and the slower ones hours or days. This lab keeps the burn rates and shrinks the windows, so the alerts fire within a few minutes.

The lab's short windows are not 1/12 of the long ones, as in the workbook: Prometheus scrapes every 30 seconds, and a rate needs at least two samples. Short windows also make alerts noisy: a minute of errors pages you. Do not use these windows in production. Step 7 shows the production ones, generated by Sloth.

The budget burn ticket fires in this lab too, but for a lab reason: the sample app is quiet, so ten minutes at 50% errors push even 12 hours of its traffic far above 0.5%. A busy service would need errors for hours before it fires.

## Step 1: Pick the SLI and send traffic

The SLI is the share of podinfo-frontend's requests that succeed. Find the metric Prometheus already scrapes from it, and see which labels say a request failed. Then run steady traffic against it: a Deployment `load` in the namespace `lab03-load` that sends about 5 requests a second to `http://podinfo-frontend.podinfo:9898/`.

Without traffic there is nothing to measure: health checks alone are a fraction of a request a second.

### Hints
- In the Prometheus UI, query `http_requests_total{namespace="podinfo"}`. The `job` label names the service, and `status` holds the HTTP code.
- A failed request is a 5xx: `status=~"5.."`. A 404 is the client's fault, so it is not an error here.
- For the load, a curl image in a loop: `while true; do curl -s -o /dev/null http://podinfo-frontend.podinfo:9898/; sleep 0.2; done`. Pin the image by digest, for example `docker.io/curlimages/curl:8.17.0@sha256:935d9100e9ba842cdb060de42472c7ca90cfe9a7c96e4dacb55e79e560b3ff40`.

### Expected result
`sum(rate(http_requests_total{namespace="podinfo",job="podinfo-frontend"}[2m]))` is above 1 request a second, and none of them fail.

## Step 2: Write recording rules for the SLI

Create the namespace `lab03-slo` and a PrometheusRule in it with four recording rules: the error ratio (failed requests over all requests) of podinfo-frontend over 2 minutes, 10 minutes, 1 hour and 12 hours, the lab windows. Name them `lab03:sli_errors:ratio_rate2m`, `lab03:sli_errors:ratio_rate10m`, `lab03:sli_errors:ratio_rate1h` and `lab03:sli_errors:ratio_rate12h`.

Recording rules compute an expression on every evaluation and store the result as a new series. Alerts on them are cheap, and every alert uses the same SLI.

### Hints
- A PrometheusRule has `spec.groups`, each with a `name` and `rules`. A recording rule has `record` and `expr`.
- The ratio: `(sum(rate(<errors>[2m])) or vector(0)) / sum(rate(<all requests>[2m]))`. Use `sum` on both sides, so the result has no labels.
- While nothing fails, there is no 5xx series at all, and a ratio over nothing has no value. `or vector(0)` makes the numerator 0 instead.
- `kubectl get --raw /api/v1/namespaces/monitoring/services/http:kps-prometheus:9090/proxy/api/v1/rules | jq` shows what Prometheus loaded.

### Expected result
In the Prometheus UI, `lab03:sli_errors:ratio_rate2m` returns one series, 0 for now.

## Step 3: Set a 99.5% SLO over 30 days

Add the SLO to the PrometheusRule: a recording rule `lab03:slo:objective_ratio` with the value `0.995` and the label `slo_window: 30d`. Then a burn rate per window, `lab03:slo:burn_rate2m` to `lab03:slo:burn_rate12h`: the error ratio divided by the error budget.

99.5% leaves an error budget of 0.5%: in 30 days, 0.5% of requests may fail. A burn rate of 1 spends the budget in exactly 30 days. A burn rate of 14.4 spends 2% of it in an hour.

### Hints
- A constant is `vector(0.995)`. Labels on a recording rule go under `labels`.
- The budget is `1 - 0.995`. `lab03:slo:burn_rate2m` is `lab03:sli_errors:ratio_rate2m / (1 - 0.995)`.
- Prometheus here keeps a day of data, so it cannot compute 30 days of anything. The SLO's period only sets how fast the budget may go.

### Expected result
`lab03:slo:objective_ratio` is 0.995, and `lab03:slo:burn_rate2m` is 0 while nothing fails.

## Step 4: Write multiwindow, multi-burn-rate alerts

Add the three alerts from the table to the PrometheusRule. `LabSLOFastBurn` fires when the burn rate is above 14.4 over both 10m and 2m, and `LabSLOSlowBurn` when it is above 6 over both 1h and 10m; both have the label `severity: page`. `LabSLOBudgetTicket` fires when it is above 1 over both 12h and 1h, with `severity: ticket`.

The long window makes an alert significant: enough budget is gone to matter. The short window makes it stop soon after the problem stops, instead of firing for an hour after the fix. A page wakes someone up, so only a burn that empties the budget in days pages; a slow, steady overspend is a ticket for working hours.

### Hints
- Both windows in one expression: `lab03:slo:burn_rate10m > 14.4 and lab03:slo:burn_rate2m > 14.4`.
- An alert has `alert`, `expr`, `labels` and `annotations` (for example a `summary`). No `for:` is needed: the short window already debounces.
- The Prometheus UI's Alerts page shows all three, inactive.

### Expected result
All three alerts are loaded and inactive.

## Step 5: Inject errors

Make podinfo fail: a Job `errors` in `lab03-load` that sends about 5 requests a second to `http://podinfo-frontend.podinfo:9898/status/500` for 10 minutes. podinfo answers that path with a 500.

With the steady load from step 1, half of all requests now fail: a burn rate of about 100. At that rate a 30-day budget lasts about 7 hours.

### Hints
- The same curl image, with a loop that stops after 600 seconds. Set `restartPolicy: Never` and `backoffLimit: 0`.
- Watch `lab03:sli_errors:ratio_rate2m` and `lab03:slo:burn_rate2m` rise in the Prometheus UI.
- The longer windows rise more slowly: an hour of mostly good traffic dilutes ten minutes of errors. That is why the slow burn alert follows the fast one.

### Expected result
`lab03:sli_errors:ratio_rate2m` is above 0.072 (7.2% = 14.4 × 0.5%).

## Step 6: Watch the fast-burn alert fire and acknowledge it

Watch `LabSLOFastBurn` go from pending to firing in Prometheus, and appear in Alertmanager. Then acknowledge the page: create a silence for `alertname=LabSLOFastBurn` for one hour, with a comment that says why.

A silence tells everyone the page is being handled, so they do not get it again while you work.

### Hints
- It fires a minute or two after the errors start: the 10m window needs that long to pass 7.2%.
- In the Alertmanager UI, the alert has a Silence button. From a shell, POST JSON to `/api/v2/silences` with `matchers`, `startsAt`, `endsAt`, `createdBy` and `comment`.
- With the port-forward from the top of this lab: `curl -H 'Content-Type: application/json' -d @silence.json http://127.0.0.1:19093/api/v2/silences`. Alertmanager takes JSON only.

### Expected result
Alertmanager shows `LabSLOFastBurn` with `severity=page`, suppressed by your silence. `LabSLOSlowBurn` (another page) and `LabSLOBudgetTicket` follow within a few minutes, once their longer windows catch up.

## Step 7: Generate the same rules with Sloth and compare

Write the SLO as a Sloth `PrometheusServiceLevel`: service `lab03-podinfo`, SLO `availability`, objective 99.5, the same error and total queries, a page and a ticket alert. Generate the rules with Sloth v0.16.0 and apply them to `lab03-slo`. Compare them with yours.

### Hints
- Sloth runs as an image: `podman run --rm -v "$PWD":/work:Z ghcr.io/slok/sloth:v0.16.0@sha256:f0f0075b0d45c1cf684e92947508cc1d5bf573925f785f803a439b2306c8b9a5 generate -i /work/slo.yaml`.
- The queries take `{{.window}}` where the range goes: `sum(rate(http_requests_total{...}[{{.window}}]))`. Sloth fills in each window.
- Given a `PrometheusServiceLevel`, Sloth writes a `PrometheusRule` in the same namespace: pipe it to `kubectl apply -f -`.

### Expected result
Prometheus loads rules with `sloth_service="lab03-podinfo"`, among them `slo:sli_error:ratio_rate5m` and a page alert. Compare:

- The burn rates are yours: 14.4 and 6 page (Sloth puts both in one page alert, joined with `or`), and 1 opens a ticket. Sloth's ticket alert also has a 3x tier over 1d and 2h, between the slow burn and the budget burn.
- The windows are the workbook's: 1h and 5m, 6h and 30m, 3d and 6h (and 1d and 2h for the 3x tier). Yours are the shorter lab windows.
- Sloth also records the SLO's metadata and the remaining error budget, which a dashboard can show.

`lab labs reset` deletes `lab03-slo` and `lab03-load`, with every rule, Deployment and Job in them, and expires the silences on `LabSLO*` alerts.
